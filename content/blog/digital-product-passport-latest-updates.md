Every few weeks a new headline says the digital product passport is here, or late, or off. Read three of them and you are less sure than when you started.

Here is the plain version. Some parts are live. Some are late. One date is fixed and close. This page sorts out which is which.

**At a glance:**

- **The plumbing is live.** The EU registry opened on 20 July 2026. Six technical standards were published in the Official Journal on 15 July 2026.
- **No ESPR product group has a passport duty yet.** The first act, on iron and steel, is expected in Q4 2026 and has not been adopted.
- **Batteries come first.** The battery passport is mandatory from **18 February 2027**.
- **One battery rule is late.** The act on who may read restricted battery data missed its 18 August 2026 deadline. A draft went out for feedback in October.
- **Other laws now carry passports too.** Detergents from 23 September 2029, toys from 1 August 2030, vehicles from 1 September 2032.

## How the passport got here

The passport rests on the [Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj), the ESPR. It is a frame. Each product group needs its own act before a passport becomes mandatory. Our [plain guide to the passport](/blog/what-is-a-digital-product-passport) covers what it holds, and our guide to [what the ESPR is](/blog/what-is-espr) covers how acts get added. The steps so far, in order:

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| 18 July 2024 | The ESPR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, enters into force. |
| 16 April 2025 | The Commission adopts the first [ESPR working plan](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52025DC0187) and names the first product groups. |
| 12 December 2025 | The [Toy Safety Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2509/oj) is published. The toy passport replaces the EU declaration of conformity. |
| 2 March 2026 | The new [Detergents Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/405/oj) is published. Its passport applies from 23 September 2029. |
| 14 to 15 July 2026 | [Decision (EU) 2026/1736](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1736/oj) is adopted and published. It cites six harmonised standards for passports. |
| 16 to 17 July 2026 | [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1778/oj) sets the rules for the registry. It enters into force on 6 August 2026. |
| 19 July 2026 | Large companies may no longer destroy unsold clothing and footwear. |
| 20 July 2026 | The [registry and a testing environment go live](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/digital-product-passport-registry-now-live-2026-07-20_en). |
| 12 August 2026 | Feedback closes on the planned iron and steel act. |
| 13 August 2026 | The [End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/waste/elv/) enters into force. It brings a vehicle passport from 1 September 2032. |
| 18 August 2026 | The legal deadline for the battery access-rights act passes without adoption. |
| October 2026 | The Commission opens feedback on a draft of that battery act. |

## What is live today

**The registry.** On 20 July 2026 the Commission opened the passport registry, with a testing environment and a helpdesk. The registry stores a passport's unique identifier, plus the commodity code for goods released for free circulation. It returns a registration identifier. That identifier is not proof that the product complies, as [Article 13 of the ESPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj) makes clear.

Nothing obliges you to register yet. No product group has a passport duty in force. Batteries are the first, on 18 February 2027.

**The standards.** [Decision (EU) 2026/1736](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1736/oj) cites six standards: data exchange protocols, unique identifiers, data carriers, data storage and persistence, application programming interfaces, and system interoperability. The Commission found that they meet the requirements in Articles 10 and 11 of the ESPR.

That is six of eight. The Commission's [batteries page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/batteries_en) describes the decision as covering six of the eight standards developed by CEN and CENELEC. The other two are still to follow.

**The destruction ban.** One ESPR duty already applies without any product act. Since 19 July 2026, large companies may not destroy unsold clothing and footwear. Medium-sized companies follow from 19 July 2030. It sits in Article 25 and Annex VII of the [ESPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj).

## What is still missing

**The first product act.** The Commission ran a call for evidence on iron and steel from 20 May to 12 August 2026 on its [Have Your Say portal](https://ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation/have-your-say_en). It now points to Q4 2026 for adoption. As of late October 2026 nothing had been adopted.

The open question is scope. The industry body Orgalim wants the passport [limited to intermediate products](https://orgalim.eu/wp-content/uploads/Orgalim-Position-Paper-ESPR-iron-steel.pdf), not the machines and parts made from steel.

**The battery access-rights act.** Article 77(9) of the [Batteries Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj) gave the Commission until 18 August 2026 to name who may read restricted battery passport data. That deadline passed. In October 2026 the Commission [opened a consultation on a draft](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/commission-consults-on-draft-implementing-regulation-on-access-rights-to-digital-battery-passports). The passport date did not move. It is still 18 February 2027.

**The service-provider act.** The Commission plans a delegated act on requirements for passport service providers. Its June 2026 timeline placed it in the first quarter of 2027. Other trackers give later dates, so treat it as 2027.

**Textiles, tyres and aluminium.** The Commission expects all three acts in Q3 to Q4 2027. Mandatory passports would follow in 2029 at the earliest, because each act applies at least 18 months after it enters into force.

**A wider product law.** The same June 2026 timeline lists proposals for a European Product Act and a Circular Economy Act for Q3 2026. We could not confirm that either has been published. Watch the Commission's page.

## FAQ

**Has the digital product passport been delayed?**
Parts of the surrounding rules have slipped. The battery access-rights act missed its 18 August 2026 deadline, and Commission timelines are indicative. The dates written into law have not moved. The battery passport is still mandatory from 18 February 2027.

Source: [EUR-Lex: Regulation (EU) 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

**Is the digital product passport registry live?**
Yes. The Commission opened it on 20 July 2026, together with a testing environment and a helpdesk. Its operating rules are in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778.

Source: [European Commission: The Digital Product Passport Registry is now live](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/digital-product-passport-registry-now-live-2026-07-20_en)

**Which product group gets an ESPR passport first?**
Iron and steel. The Commission expects the delegated act in Q4 2026. It had not been adopted as of late October 2026. Each ESPR act applies at least 18 months after it enters into force.

Source: [European Commission: ESPR and Energy Labelling Working Plan 2025-2030](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52025DC0187)

**Do I have to register a passport in the registry today?**
No. No product group has a passport duty in force yet. Batteries are first, from 18 February 2027.

Source: [EUR-Lex: Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, Article 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781/oj)

**Which standards support the digital product passport?**
Six harmonised standards, EN 18216 and EN 18219 to EN 18223, cover data exchange, unique identifiers, data carriers, data storage, APIs and system interoperability. The Commission published their references on 15 July 2026.

Source: [EUR-Lex: Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1736](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2026/1736/oj)

**Where can I check for the latest changes?**
The European Commission's digital product passport page, the Official Journal on EUR-Lex, and the Commission's Have Your Say portal for drafts open to feedback.

Source: [European Commission: Digital Product Passport](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport_en)
