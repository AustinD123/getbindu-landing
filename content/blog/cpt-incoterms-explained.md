Your seller quotes "CPT Hamburg". You read that as "they bring it to Hamburg". So the risk is theirs until it arrives. Right?

Wrong, and it is a common misreading of the term. Under CPT the seller pays for the transport, but the risk passes to you much earlier. This is the checklist we would run before you agree CPT. Seven checks, in the order they come up.

**At a glance:**

- CPT stands for **Carriage Paid To**. The seller delivers the goods to a carrier it names, and **pays the carriage to the named destination**.
- **Risk passes to the buyer when the goods are handed to the carrier**, well before they arrive.
- The **seller clears the goods for export**. The **buyer handles import**.
- CPT has **no insurance obligation**. CIP is the term that adds insurance.
- CPT works for **any mode of transport**, including air, road, rail, sea and combinations.

## 1. Know what CPT means

German customs defines CPT in one line: the seller delivers the goods to the carrier it names and contracts and pays for the carriage to the named destination. The [ICC Academy](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-cpt-or-cip/), which belongs to the body that publishes the rules, adds that CPT applies to any mode of transport, including sea, road, rail, air or a combination.

## 2. Keep two places apart: delivery and destination

This is where most mistakes start. Under the C-terms the contract names the **destination**, but delivery happens at a different place, often in the seller's country. The ICC calls the belief that risk passes at the destination a common but critical mistake. Its example: for goods sold "CPT Dubai", risk passes when the goods are handed to the carrier at the departure airport. The seller pays for the flight. Damage after the handover is the buyer's problem.

So agree both places in writing: where the goods are handed to the carrier, and where they must be carried to.

## 3. Know when delivery really happens

Delivery is the handover to the carrier. According to the ICC, it does not happen when the goods are loaded onto the aircraft, train or vessel. If the transport uses several modes, such as truck, vessel and train, delivery happens when the goods go to the first carrier. The seller still pays the transport to the destination, however many modes there are.

## 4. Split the paperwork correctly

The seller completes export formalities. The buyer is responsible for import formalities and for customs procedures in any country the goods pass through. The seller must also meet the transport security requirements, at its own cost and risk. If you are the buyer, the import side is yours from the start: book your customs agent early.

## 5. Decide who insures the goods

CPT does not oblige anyone to insure the cargo. The ICC notes that insurance is covered in only two of the eleven rules, CIP and CIF. Under CPT the buyer carries the risk from the handover, so the buyer should look at its own cargo cover. Ask your insurer or broker what applies to the route.

## 6. Compare CPT with CIP and DAP

CPT sits in the middle. The seller pays freight, but the risk passes early. CIP adds insurance paid by the seller. According to German customs, CIP is CPT plus insurance at the level of Institute Cargo Clauses A. DAP delivers to the named place of destination, ready for unloading, and the risk passes there.

| | CPT | CIP | DAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who pays the main carriage | Seller | Seller | Seller |
| Where risk passes | Handover to first carrier | Handover to first carrier | Named destination |
| Insurance obligation | None | Seller insures | None |
| Export clearance | Seller | Seller | Seller |
| Import clearance | Buyer | Buyer | Buyer |

For DAP in detail, see [DAP vs DDP](/blog/dap-vs-ddp-incoterms).

## 7. Tell your customs agent how the freight is priced

For goods entering the EU, German customs says transport and insurance costs are added to the price up to the place where the goods enter the EU, if they are not already in it. Under CPT the price already carries the freight to the named destination. Your agent still has to work out which part of it counts up to the EU border, so send the carrier's freight details with the invoice. **Where does risk pass under CPT?**
When the goods are handed to the carrier at the agreed place of delivery. The named destination comes later.

**Does CPT include insurance?**
No. Insurance is covered in only two of the eleven Incoterms rules, CIP and CIF.

**What is the difference between CPT and CIP?**
CIP is CPT plus insurance paid by the seller. According to German customs, the cover is at the level of Institute Cargo Clauses A.

**Who handles customs under CPT?**
The seller completes export formalities. The buyer handles import formalities and customs in any transit country.
