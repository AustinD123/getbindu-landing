Your supplier quotes "EXW" and a low price. Then the questions start. Who loads the truck? Who files the export declaration? And who counts as the exporter on paper?

EXW looks like the simplest term in the book. For the buyer, it is the one with the most work hidden in it. This is the checklist we would run before you accept it. Seven checks, in the order they come up.

**At a glance:**

- EXW stands for **Ex Works**. The seller makes the goods available at its premises or another named place.
- The seller **does not have to load** the goods onto your truck and **does not have to clear them for export**.
- The **buyer collects, loads, pays the transport and handles export formalities**.
- The ICC, which publishes the rules, says EXW suits **domestic trade** best and encourages **FCA** when goods cross a border.
- The price **excludes transport**, which matters for the customs value when you import into the EU.

## 1. Know what EXW means

German customs defines EXW in one sentence: the seller delivers by making the goods available at its premises or another named place. The seller need not load them onto the collecting vehicle and need not clear them for export. The [ICC Academy](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-exw-or-fca/) says EXW places the minimum obligation on the seller and works for any mode of transport.

## 2. Name the place exactly, and write the term in full

Risk passes to the buyer when the goods are ready to be loaded onto the vehicle you send. So the place matters. Write the rule, the place and the version: "EXW Seller's warehouse, Mumbai, Incoterms 2020". "EXW India" tells nobody where the risk changed hands.

## 3. Plan the loading

According to the ICC, loading the goods onto the buyer's vehicle is done at the buyer's expense and risk. The seller only has to make them available. Before the truck leaves, check that your carrier can load at that site, with the right equipment and a pick-up time the seller has agreed in writing.

## 4. Ask who clears export, and whether they can

Under EXW the buyer is responsible for export formalities. The ICC warns that this can cause complications for both sides. Its example: a seller may be treated as the exporter of record if the buyer is not established in the seller's country. If your side cannot lodge an export declaration there, EXW is the wrong term. Ask this before you sign, not at the gate.

## 5. Do not use EXW when you mean FCA

The two terms look alike. They are not. Under FCA the seller delivers to the carrier you name and clears the goods for export. The ICC strongly encourages FCA instead of EXW where goods cross a border.

| | EXW | FCA |
|---|---|---|
| Where it is delivered | Seller's premises or named place | Carrier named by buyer, at premises or named place |
| Loading by seller | No | At premises, onto the collecting vehicle |
| Export clearance | Buyer | Seller |
| ICC view for cross-border trade | Complications possible, FCA encouraged | Suits any mode, including containers |

For the full picture of the alternative, read [FCA Incoterms explained](/blog/fca-incoterms-explained).

## 6. Work out the customs value before you import

For goods entering the EU, German customs says transport and insurance costs are added to the price up to the place where the goods enter the EU, if they are not already in it. An EXW price contains none of them. So the freight from the seller's door to the EU border, plus any insurance, goes on top. Ask your customs agent to set the value before the goods ship. ## 7. Keep the paperwork moving

Your carrier arrives, loads and leaves. The seller's invoice and packing list have to be ready at that moment, because the export side and your import side both start from them. Agree the pick-up window, ask for both documents the same day, and check that product names, quantities and weights match on each.

## FAQ

**What does EXW stand for?**
Ex Works. The seller delivers by making the goods available at its premises or another named place.

Source: [Zoll: Lieferbedingungen (Incoterms 2020)](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Zoelle/Zollwert/Methoden-der-Zollwertermittlung/Transaktionswert-fuer-die-eingefuehrte-Ware/Berichtigungen-Artikel-71-72-UZK/Befoerderungs-und-Versicherungskosten/Lieferbedingungen/lieferbedingung.html)

**Who pays for export clearance under EXW?**
The buyer is responsible for export formalities. The seller does not have to clear the goods for export.

Source: [ICC Academy: EXW and FCA explained](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-exw-or-fca/)

**Where does risk pass under EXW?**
At the seller's premises or other named place, once the goods are available and ready for loading onto the vehicle the buyer sends.

Source: [ICC Academy: EXW and FCA explained](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-exw-or-fca/)

**Can I use EXW for international shipments?**
Technically yes. The ICC says export complications can arise and strongly encourages FCA when goods cross a border.

Source: [ICC Academy: EXW and FCA explained](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-exw-or-fca/)

**Is EXW the same as FCA?**
No. Under EXW the seller need not load the goods or clear them for export. Under FCA the seller must clear them for export.

Source: [Zoll: Lieferbedingungen (Incoterms 2020)](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Zoelle/Zollwert/Methoden-der-Zollwertermittlung/Transaktionswert-fuer-die-eingefuehrte-Ware/Berichtigungen-Artikel-71-72-UZK/Befoerderungs-und-Versicherungskosten/Lieferbedingungen/lieferbedingung.html)
