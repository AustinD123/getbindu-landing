A container of frozen fish is packed and the booking is made. Your buyer in Europe writes: the catch certificate does not match the invoice. Now the container waits, and the person who waits longest is you.

Most of these problems start before the container is loaded. This is the checklist we would run on every EU-bound fish shipment from India. Ten checks, in the order you will meet them. It covers the MPEDA catch certificate, the processing statement and the EU's CATCH system.

**At a glance:**

- Wild-caught fishery products need a **catch certificate validated by the flag State** of the vessel. In India, **MPEDA validates** it for seafood exported to the EU.
- Farmed shrimp raised from larvae is **excluded**. Check your tariff line against Annex I.
- MPEDA's fee is **Rs 3,000 plus 18% GST for frozen** and **Rs 1,500 plus GST for chilled, dried or live** cargo.
- Since **10 January 2026**, EU importers file through the **CATCH** system. You can still send paper.
- Most mismatches come from the same facts typed into three documents.

## 1. Check whether your product needs a certificate

Wild-caught marine products need one: wild tuna, squid, cuttlefish, sea-caught finfish. Annex I of [Regulation (EC) No 1005/2008](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02008R1005-20260110) excludes aquaculture products from fry or larvae and many freshwater species. Pond-raised shrimp from larvae needs none.

The catch is in the detail. Annex I works by product description and tariff line, and many entries are partial codes. A farmed product and a wild-caught one can sit under the same heading. Check your exact code and confirm how the product was sourced. [How to read an HS code](/blog/what-is-an-hs-code) explains why partial matches trip people up.

## 2. Confirm which flag State validates

The certificate follows the catch, not the exporter. The flag State of the vessel validates it. For an Indian-flagged vessel, that is India, and MPEDA is the validating body. If part of your catch came from a vessel with another flag, that catch needs validation from its own flag State. Ask your supplier which vessels are in each lot before you apply.

## 3. Register with MPEDA

Exporters of marine products register with MPEDA. That is a separate step from the catch certificate, with its own fee. Confirm your registration is in place before you apply for the first certificate.

## 4. Apply on the MPEDA portal, and ask for the procedure

MPEDA runs a dedicated catch certificate portal. When we checked it on 11 November 2026, the login page offered four roles: exporter, field officer, HDC and MPEDA. Two parts were not usable. The procedure link on the home page went to a placeholder, and the FAQ page read "coming soon". So ask your MPEDA regional office for the current procedure before your first filing. MPEDA's [catch certificate page](https://mpeda.gov.in/?page_id=947) is the starting point.

## 5. Match the product details across documents

The species, product form and tariff line should be the same on the catch certificate, the invoice and the packing list. A buyer's customs agent will compare them. If the certificate says "squid" and the invoice says "cephalopod, frozen", expect a question.

## 6. Make the quantities add up

The weight on the certificate, the processing statement and the invoice should reconcile. A processed product weighs less than the raw material it came from, so the statement has to show both. Since CATCH went live, the weight of raw material imported under one certificate cannot exceed the weight validated in it (Article 12b). If a certificate says 20 tonnes, shipments drawing on it cannot add up to more.

## 7. Add the processing statement, if you process

If the product is processed, you need a second document: a statement from the processing plant in the form of Annex IV (Article 14(2)). It describes the unprocessed and processed products and their quantities, and confirms the processed goods come from catches covered by validated certificates. The competent authority of the processing country endorses it. We did not find which Indian body endorses the Annex IV statement, so ask MPEDA which office handles it for your plant.

## 8. Match the transport details

The importer submits the transport details from the Appendix to Annex II with the certificate (Article 16(1)). They have to match the shipment as it actually travels: vessel, container, route. A change after filing, such as a swapped container or a rolled booking, can leave the documents out of line with the cargo.

## 9. Get the certificate to your buyer in time

Since 10 January 2026, [EU importers must file through CATCH](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-10231-2025-INIT/en/pdf). The importer files at least three working days before arrival, and the deadline can be adapted by product, distance or transport. That deadline is your buyer's, but you feel it. If your certificate arrives late, their filing slips and your container waits. As an Indian exporter you do not have to use CATCH yourself. Paper certificates are still accepted, and the importer enters the data.

## 10. Keep your own record of each certificate

Because quantities are now tracked, keep a running record of how much of each certificate you have shipped. Certificates validated before 10 January 2026 can still be used by importers until 10 January 2028, according to [AGRINFO](https://agrinfo.eu/book-of-reports/revised-eu-rules-and-digitalisation-of-fisheries-control/), so old paper may still be in circulation. A one-page tracker per certificate is enough.

## Before you ship

1. Run checks 1 to 3 once for each product line.
2. Run checks 5, 6 and 8 for every container.
3. Send the importer the certificate and the processing statement together.
4. Confirm the current MPEDA procedure and the CATCH filing with your buyer before you load.

## FAQ

**What is an EU catch certificate?**
It is a document saying a catch was made in line with the laws and conservation rules that apply to the vessel. Fishery products may only enter the EU with a catch certificate validated by the flag State.

Source: [EUR-Lex: Regulation (EC) No 1005/2008](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02008R1005-20260110)

**Who issues the catch certificate in India?**
MPEDA, the Marine Products Export Development Authority. It describes itself as the nodal agency for validating catch certificates for seafood exported to the EU.

Source: [MPEDA: Catch certificate](https://mpeda.gov.in/?page_id=947)

**Does farmed shrimp need an EU catch certificate?**
Not if it was raised from fry or larvae. Annex I excludes aquaculture products obtained from fry or larvae. Check your exact tariff line.

Source: [EUR-Lex: Regulation (EC) No 1005/2008, Annex I](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02008R1005-20260110)

**How much does an MPEDA catch certificate cost?**
Rs 3,000 plus 18% GST for frozen cargo, and Rs 1,500 plus 18% GST for chilled, dried or live cargo. Check MPEDA's fee page before you pay.

Source: [MPEDA: Certificate fee](https://mpeda.gov.in/?page_id=1029)

**Do I need to use the CATCH system as an Indian exporter?**
No. CATCH has been mandatory for EU importers and authorities since 10 January 2026. Paper certificates continue to be accepted, and your importer files the data.

Source: [Council of the EU: Mandatory use of CATCH](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-10231-2025-INIT/en/pdf)
