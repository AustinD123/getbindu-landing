Your buyer in Europe writes "FCA, please". Do you load the truck? Who clears export? And where exactly does the risk pass: at your warehouse door, or at the port?

FCA is a short term with a lot packed into it. This is the checklist we would run before you agree it. Eight checks, in the order they come up.

**At a glance:**

- FCA stands for **Free Carrier**. The seller delivers the goods to the **carrier named by the buyer**, at the seller's premises or at another named place.
- **Risk passes to the buyer at that place**, so the place has to be exact.
- The **seller clears the goods for export**.
- The **buyer arranges and pays the main transport**. FCA works for any mode of transport, including containers.
- With FCA the price **does not include freight**, which matters for the importer's customs value.

## 1. Know what FCA means

German customs defines FCA in one sentence: the seller delivers the goods to the carrier, or another person the buyer names, at the seller's premises or another named place. It adds that the seller must clear the goods for export, where that applies. The [ICC Academy](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-fca-or-fob/), the body that publishes the rules, adds that FCA suits any mode of transport, including multimodal shipments, and fits container transport well.

## 2. Name the place exactly, and write the term in full

Risk passes at the point where you hand over the goods. German customs advises naming that point inside the delivery place as precisely as possible. So write the rule, the place and the version, the way the ICC does: "FCA Seller's factory, Zhengzhou, Incoterms 2020". "FCA China" tells nobody where the risk changed hands.

## 3. Decide: your premises or a terminal

Under FCA the parties choose one of two delivery points. At the seller's premises, the ICC says the seller has delivered once the goods are loaded onto the buyer's collecting vehicle. At another agreed place, such as an airport cargo terminal, a rail terminal or a container terminal at the port of shipment, the seller delivers by handing the goods to the carrier, ready for unloading from the seller's own arriving vehicle. In the first case you load. In the second you do not.

## 4. Remember that export clearance is yours

Whichever delivery point you pick, the seller completes the export formalities and the security requirements for transport. If you ship from India, that means the export side is on you even though the buyer pays for the main carriage. Plan the paperwork before the truck arrives, not when it is at the gate.

## 5. Do not use FCA when you mean EXW

EXW is a pure collection term. According to German customs, the seller delivers by making the goods available at its premises or another named place. It need not load them onto the collecting vehicle and need not clear them for export. If you want the buyer to handle export clearance, EXW is the term. If you will clear export, use FCA.

## 6. Do not use FCA when you mean FOB

FOB delivers the goods on board the vessel at the named port, and the ICC says it is for maritime or inland waterway transport only. FCA is the appropriate rule for containers and pallets and for several modes of transport. The ICC adds a warning on FOB: if the buyer is late with the vessel name or the loading point, the buyer carries the risk even before the goods are on board.

| | EXW | FCA | FOB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where it is delivered | Seller's premises or named place | Carrier named by buyer, at premises or named place | On board the vessel at the named port |
| Loading by seller | No | At premises, onto the collecting vehicle | On board |
| Export clearance | Buyer | Seller | Seller |
| Transport modes | Any | Any | Sea and inland waterway only |

## 7. Agree who books the carrier, and what the invoice covers

The buyer names the carrier and contracts for the main transport. Agree the carrier's name and the pick-up time in writing, because your delivery obligation depends on handing the goods to that carrier. The ICC notes that under FCA the commercial invoice covers only the cost of the goods plus any export clearance and documentation costs. Freight is not on it.

## 8. Tell your EU buyer about the customs value

For goods entering the EU, German customs says transport and insurance costs are added to the price up to the place where the goods enter the EU, if they are not already in it. Under FCA the price excludes freight, so your buyer's agent will need the freight costs to set the customs value. Send the carrier's invoice or freight details with the shipment. ## FAQ

**What does FCA stand for?**
Free Carrier. The seller delivers the goods to the carrier named by the buyer at the seller's premises or another named place.

Source: [Zoll: Lieferbedingungen (Incoterms 2020)](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Zoelle/Zollwert/Methoden-der-Zollwertermittlung/Transaktionswert-fuer-die-eingefuehrte-Ware/Berichtigungen-Artikel-71-72-UZK/Befoerderungs-und-Versicherungskosten/Lieferbedingungen/lieferbedingung.html)

**Where does risk pass under FCA?**
At the agreed delivery place: the seller's premises once the goods are loaded onto the buyer's collecting vehicle, or another named place once the goods are handed to the carrier.

Source: [ICC Academy: FCA and FOB explained](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-fca-or-fob/)

**Who pays for freight under FCA?**
The buyer contracts for and pays the main transport. The seller's invoice covers the goods and any export clearance and documentation costs.

Source: [ICC Academy: FCA and FOB explained](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-fca-or-fob/)

**Is FCA the same as EXW?**
No. Under EXW the seller need not load the goods or clear them for export. Under FCA the seller must clear them for export.

Source: [Zoll: Lieferbedingungen (Incoterms 2020)](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Zoelle/Zollwert/Methoden-der-Zollwertermittlung/Transaktionswert-fuer-die-eingefuehrte-Ware/Berichtigungen-Artikel-71-72-UZK/Befoerderungs-und-Versicherungskosten/Lieferbedingungen/lieferbedingung.html)

**When should I use FCA instead of FOB?**
When goods go in containers or on pallets, or by more than one mode of transport. FOB is only for sea and inland waterway transport.

Source: [ICC Academy: FCA and FOB explained](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-fca-or-fob/)
