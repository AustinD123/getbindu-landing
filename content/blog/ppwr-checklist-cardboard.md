Your packaging is cardboard, so you may think the EU packaging rule is mostly a plastics problem. Some of it is. But the PPWR has applied since 12 August 2026, and the parts that reach a cardboard brand are already in force.

This is a checklist for a brand that sells in cardboard boxes, mailers and cartons. It sorts what to do now from what can wait until 2028 and later, so you spend your time on the right ones.

**At a glance:**

- The PPWR is [Regulation (EU) 2025/40](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/40/oj/eng). Most core duties have applied since **12 August 2026**.
- Cardboard escapes the recycled-content rule, which covers plastic parts. It does not escape substance limits, the declaration of conformity or the technical documentation.
- Harmonised labels follow from **12 August 2028**. Recyclability grades and the empty-space cap come later, from 2030 at the earliest.
- If you put your own name on a box someone else made, you are the manufacturer (Article 21).

## Do now: in force since 12 August 2026

### 1. Work out your role

The PPWR stacks duties by role, and one company can hold several. The **manufacturer** (Article 15) designs or has the packaging made and holds the technical documentation. The **producer** (Article 44) first makes packaging available in a given country and carries the registration and producer-responsibility duties there.

The trap is Article 21. If you buy plain boxes and print your own brand on them, you become the manufacturer. Your converter built the box. The duty is still yours. Our [PPWR explainer](/blog/what-is-ppwr) lays out who carries which duty.

### 2. List your packaging types

Count packaging types. A packaging type is a distinct combination of material, closure and decoration. Forty products in the same printed mailer are one type. The same product in a retail carton, a shipping box and an e-commerce mailer is three.

Group by format too: sales packaging, grouped packaging, transport packaging and e-commerce packaging. The format decides which rules reach each item.

### 3. Ask your suppliers about substances

Article 5 limits hazardous substances in packaging. Article 5(5) restricts PFAS in food-contact packaging. If your cardboard holds food, a grease-resistant coating is the first thing to question. Ask your converter in writing what is in the board, the coating, the ink and the adhesive. Keep the answer.

### 4. Draw up a declaration of conformity per packaging type

Each packaging type needs its own written declaration, on the model in Annex VIII, backed by technical documentation (Annex VII). You do not file it anywhere. You hold it and hand it over if an authority asks, in practice within about 10 days. It has to be kept up to date, so a new ink or a new board supplier can reopen it. Our [declaration of conformity guide](/blog/ppwr-declaration-of-conformity) walks through the fields.

### 5. Register where you sell

Producers register nationally as a precondition to selling. The rules and the register differ by country, so check each market you ship to. One proposal to watch: the Council's position of 21 October 2026 on the Environmental Omnibus would suspend the requirement to appoint an authorised representative for producer responsibility. It is only a proposal. Plan as if the current rule stands.

## Ask your board and print suppliers

### 6. Check coatings, laminates and barriers

Recyclability is assessed on the components. How the box looks decides nothing. A plain corrugated box is straightforward. A box with a plastic lamination, a window or a barrier coating is a different case. The grades arrive later, but the design choice you make today is the one that gets graded.

### 7. Look for plastic parts

Cardboard brands often have plastic in the box without noticing: tape, a film window, a poly mailer insert, a protective sleeve. The recycled-content rule applies to the plastic parts. Each one is its own component in your assessment. List them all.

### 8. Right-size your boxes

The PPWR requires packaging to be no larger or heavier than it needs to be. For grouped, transport and e-commerce packaging, the empty space ratio is capped at 50 percent from 2030. If you are about to order new tooling or a new box size, that is the cheap moment to design for it.

## Can wait: 2028 and later

### 9. Labels, from 12 August 2028

The harmonised label tells the consumer what the packaging is made of and how to sort it. It applies from 12 August 2028, or 24 months after the Commission adopts the implementing act, whichever is later. As far as we could find, the act that sets the label design has not been adopted yet. Do not print new labels on a guess. Wait for the specification, and budget artwork time for 2028. Transport packaging is exempt from this label. E-commerce packaging is not. [Labelling requirements in detail](/blog/ppwr-labelling-requirements).

### 10. Grades, the empty-space cap and the 2038 cut-off

Put these later dates in the calendar:

- **2030 at the earliest:** recyclability grades (A, B and C), the minimum recycled content in plastic parts, and the empty-space ratio.
- **1 January 2038:** grade C packaging can no longer be placed on the EU market. You will see 2035 quoted. That is when a recycled-at-scale factor joins the grade, a different event.

Nothing to file for these today. The work is to avoid design choices that lock you into a grade C box.

## FAQ

**Does the PPWR apply to cardboard packaging?**
Yes. The PPWR covers all packaging placed on the EU market, whatever the material. The recycled-content rule covers plastic parts only, so a plain cardboard box falls outside it. Substance limits, documentation and the declaration of conformity still apply.

Source: [EUR-Lex: PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/40/oj/eng)

**When does the PPWR start to apply?**
It entered into force on 11 February 2025 and applies from 12 August 2026. Harmonised labelling follows from 12 August 2028.

Source: [European Commission: Packaging and packaging waste](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/waste-and-recycling/packaging-waste_en)

**Do I need a declaration of conformity for each box?**
You need one per packaging type, not per product. A type is a distinct combination of material, closure and decoration. A different box or a different closure is a different type.

Source: [EUR-Lex: PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/40/oj/eng)

**Does a brand that only buys boxes count as a manufacturer?**
Not if the box is sold as the converter made it. If the brand puts its own name or trademark on packaging, or modifies it, Article 21 makes the brand the manufacturer.

Source: [EUR-Lex: PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/40/oj/eng)

**What can wait until 2028 or later?**
The harmonised label (from 12 August 2028), recyclability grades and the empty-space ratio (2030 at the earliest), and the end of grade C packaging on the EU market (1 January 2038).

Source: [EUR-Lex: PPWR, Regulation (EU) 2025/40](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/40/oj/eng)
