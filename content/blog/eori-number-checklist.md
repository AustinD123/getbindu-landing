A first shipment is booked, the invoice is ready, and then the customs agent asks for your EORI number. If you have never heard the term, that question can stop a container for days.

The good news: the EORI number is free, it does not expire, and for many exporters outside the EU the answer is "you do not need one". This is the checklist we would run before the first shipment. Eight checks, in the order you meet them.

**At a glance:**

- An EORI number identifies you to EU customs. It is **mandatory for customs operations in the EU**: import, export and transit.
- A company **established in the EU** needs one. A company **outside the EU** needs one only if it lodges declarations itself or acts as a carrier.
- **One person, one EORI.** It has **no expiry date**.
- It is issued by **one national customs authority**, for free.
- Your **VAT ID is not your EORI**.

## 1. Check whether you need one at all

The European Commission says an EORI number is [mandatory for customs clearance in the EU](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/customs-procedures-import-and-export/customs-operations/economic-operators-registration-and-identification-number-eori_en): imports, exports and transit. Any operator established in the EU customs territory needs one.

An operator established outside the EU needs one only when it does certain things itself: lodging customs declarations, entry or exit summary declarations, or temporary storage declarations, or acting as a carrier. For an exporter in India, that usually comes down to one question: who lodges the import declaration in the EU? If your buyer or their customs agent does, you normally do not need an EORI for that shipment. Ask them in writing before you spend time on an application.

## 2. Check that you do not already have one

A person can hold only one valid EORI number. Before you apply, check whether your company already has one, for example from an earlier import or from a sister company's registration. The Commission offers an EORI validation tool where you can look up a number, or check a name and address. A second application only creates confusion.

## 3. Pick the right country to apply in

An EORI number is issued by a single customs authority. For a company established in the EU, that is the national customs authority of the country where it is established. A non-EU company applies in the EU country where it first lodges a declaration or applies for a customs decision. If it has several permanent establishments in the EU, it can choose any of those countries.

The number then works across the whole EU. You do not need one per country.

## 4. Know what the number looks like

An EORI number starts with the two-letter code of the country that issued it, followed by an identifier that is unique in that country, up to 15 alphanumeric characters. If a number you are given has no country prefix, or the wrong one for the company, ask for a corrected one before it goes on a declaration.

## 5. Apply, and do not pay for it

Applying for an EORI number is free of charge. German customs says paid third-party services are not needed. Each country runs its own application route, so go to the customs authority of the country you chose in check 3.

In Germany, the route changed this autumn. According to [German customs](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Zoelle/EORI-Nummer/Beantragung-einer-EORI-Nummer/beantragung-einer-eori-nummer_node.html), applying for or ending an EORI number and changing master data must go through the Zoll-Portal from 1 October 2026. The paper form 0870 is only a fallback when the portal is unavailable. The portal login needs an ELSTER login for the company or person, and that can take time, so create it first.

## 6. Apply before the first customs activity

The number has to exist before the activity, not after it. Apply before your first declaration, summary declaration or carrier role, and keep a copy of your application and any confirmation. Do not let your agent start a declaration with a number that is still pending.

## 7. Validate the numbers of your partners

You are not only checking your own number. Importer, exporter, agent and carrier numbers all end up on customs documents. Before a shipment, run each EORI through the Commission's validation tool. It tells you whether a number is valid. German customs notes that the name and address of a holder are visible only with the holder's consent, so a missing name is not proof of a wrong number.

## 8. Keep it current, and do not confuse it with other numbers

An EORI number has no expiry date. It can be invalidated on request, or when the business stops operating, and the data is then kept for 10 years. If your address or legal details change, update your master data with the issuing authority.

An EORI is not a VAT ID, a tax number or an excise number. German customs lists the Umsatzsteuer-ID, the Steuernummer and the Verbrauchsteuernummer as separate things. ## FAQ

**What does EORI stand for?**
Economic Operators Registration and Identification. It is a number that identifies businesses and other persons in their dealings with EU customs.

Source: [European Commission: EORI](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/customs-procedures-import-and-export/customs-operations/economic-operators-registration-and-identification-number-eori_en)

**Does an exporter in India need an EORI number?**
Only if it lodges customs declarations, entry or exit summary declarations or temporary storage declarations in the EU, or acts as a carrier. If the EU importer or its agent lodges the declaration, the exporter normally does not.

Source: [European Commission: EORI](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/customs-procedures-import-and-export/customs-operations/economic-operators-registration-and-identification-number-eori_en)

**How much does an EORI number cost?**
Nothing. In Germany, customs states that applying is free and that you do not need paid third-party services.

Source: [Zoll: EORI-Nummer](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Zoelle/EORI-Nummer/eori-nummer_node.html)

**Does an EORI number expire?**
No. It has no expiry date. It can be invalidated on request or when the business activity ends, and the data is then kept for 10 years.

Source: [European Commission: EORI](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/customs-procedures-import-and-export/customs-operations/economic-operators-registration-and-identification-number-eori_en)

**Is an EORI number the same as a VAT number?**
No. They are separate identifiers with separate purposes. Customs lists the EORI apart from the Umsatzsteuer-ID, the Steuernummer and the Verbrauchsteuernummer.

Source: [Zoll: EORI-Nummer](https://www.zoll.de/DE/Fachthemen/Zoelle/EORI-Nummer/eori-nummer_node.html)
