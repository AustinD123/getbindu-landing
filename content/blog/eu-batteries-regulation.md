Nobody used to ask where the lithium in a battery came from, or how much carbon went into making it. Since 18 February 2024, the EU does.

The EU Batteries Regulation treats a battery as a product with a history you have to prove, from the mine to the recycling bin.

If you make, import, or sell batteries in the EU, this is now the rulebook. Not every duty applies yet, and the dates are not all where they were a year ago.

**At a glance:**

- The rulebook is Regulation (EU) 2023/1542. It applied from 18 February 2024 and replaced the 2006 Batteries Directive.
- It sorts every battery into five categories: portable, LMT, industrial, electric-vehicle and SLI.
- On **18 February 2027** every battery needs a QR code. LMT batteries, industrial batteries above 2 kWh and electric-vehicle batteries also need a battery passport.
- Supply-chain due diligence now applies from **18 August 2027**, not 2025. Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 moved it.
- A Commission proposal to simplify the rules, COM(2025) 981, is still open. It is not law.

## What is the EU Batteries Regulation?

**The EU Batteries Regulation** is [Regulation (EU) 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng), the single law that governs every battery placed on the EU market. It entered into force on 17 August 2023. It has applied since 18 February 2024, replacing the old 2006 Batteries Directive.

The shift from a directive to a regulation is the whole story. A directive is written into each country's own law. A regulation applies directly and identically across the EU. So this is one battery rulebook for the entire market.

It covers the full life of a battery: how it is made, what goes into it, how it performs, and what happens when it dies. Annex XIII then sets out [the data the passport has to carry](/blog/battery-passport-data). German readers can find the same ground in our guide to the [Batterieverordnung](/de/blog/batterieverordnung).

## Who does it apply to?

The regulation sorts batteries into categories: portable, LMT, industrial, electric-vehicle, and SLI (starting, lighting and ignition). Different duties attach to different categories, so the first job is knowing which box a battery sits in.

**LMT batteries** are batteries that power light means of transport, such as e-bikes and e-scooters. **SLI batteries** are the starting, lighting and ignition batteries that most vehicles carry.

**An economic operator** is the manufacturer, importer or distributor that places a battery on the EU market. That operator carries the duties. If you bring batteries across the EU border, you are the importer and the obligations are yours. You cannot pass them back up the chain to a supplier outside the bloc.

## What does it actually require?

The regulation is a stack of duties that ride on the battery itself, not a single form. Each has its own evidence and its own date.

| Duty | Applies to | From |
|---|---|---|
| Separate collection symbol | All batteries | 18 August 2025 |
| Labels with general information and capacity | Depends on the battery type | 18 August 2026, or 18 months after the Commission's implementing act, whichever is later |
| QR code | All batteries | 18 February 2027 |
| Battery passport | LMT batteries, industrial batteries above 2 kWh, electric-vehicle batteries | 18 February 2027 |
| Removable and replaceable batteries | Portable and LMT batteries | 18 February 2027 |
| Supply-chain due diligence | Operators above the turnover threshold | 18 August 2027 |
| Recycled-content documentation | Industrial batteries above 2 kWh, electric-vehicle and SLI batteries | 18 August 2028, or 24 months after the delegated act, whichever is later |
| Recycled-content minimums | Same categories | 18 August 2031 |

The dates come from Articles 8, 11, 13, 48, 77 and 96 of the [regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng), and, for due diligence, from [Regulation (EU) 2025/1561](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/1561/oj).

**Carbon footprint.** Electric-vehicle batteries, rechargeable industrial batteries above 2 kWh and LMT batteries need a carbon footprint declaration, calculated on a set method (Article 7). The fixed dates are staggered: 18 February 2025 for electric-vehicle batteries, 18 February 2026 for industrial batteries, 18 August 2028 for LMT batteries. Each is the later of that date and 12 or 18 months after the Commission's act on the method. We could not confirm that those acts are adopted, so check the Commission's [batteries page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/waste-and-recycling/batteries_en) before you set a date.

**Labelling and QR.** From 18 February 2027 every battery carries a QR code. On LMT batteries, industrial batteries above 2 kWh and electric-vehicle batteries it opens the battery passport. On other batteries it opens the label information, the declaration of conformity and the waste-handling details. The general labels follow the same "later of" rule as in the table. We could not confirm that the implementing act behind them is in force.

**Collection and recycling.** There are targets for collecting waste batteries and recovering materials. Portable batteries must be removable and replaceable by the user, and LMT batteries by an independent professional.

## Supply-chain due diligence

**Supply-chain due diligence** is the duty to check and manage the social and environmental risks tied to the raw materials in a battery. It is not a one-off form. It is an ongoing policy that a notified body verifies and audits (Articles 48 and 51). The raw materials in focus are cobalt, lithium, nickel and natural graphite.

The date has moved. It first applied from 18 August 2025. [Regulation (EU) 2025/1561](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/1561/oj) pushed it to **18 August 2027**, and moved the deadline for the Commission's guidelines to 26 July 2026. It did not change the passport date.

Not everyone is in scope. The chapter does not apply to operators with a net turnover below EUR 40 million in the financial year before last, unless they sit in a group above that line (Article 47). A further package would widen that exemption. More on that below.

This is the same shape as the rest of modern [trade compliance](/blog/what-is-trade-compliance): the product has to carry proof of how it was made.

## Which batteries carry which duties

The three headline duties map onto the five categories like this:

| Battery category | Carbon footprint | Recycled content | Battery passport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portable | No | No | No |
| LMT | Yes | No | Yes |
| Industrial | Yes (rechargeable, above 2 kWh) | Yes (above 2 kWh) | Yes (above 2 kWh) |
| Electric-vehicle | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| SLI | No | Yes | No |

Labelling and the QR code sit outside this table. They reach across the categories. So does the collection and recycling regime.

## The recycled-content minimums

The recycled-content rule targets four metals. Each battery in scope has to prove it holds at least this share of recycled material from 18 August 2031:

| Metal | Minimum recycled share from 2031 |
|---|---|
| Cobalt | 16% |
| Lead | 85% |
| Lithium | 6% |
| Nickel | 6% |

These are floors, not targets, and they rise further from 2036. The rule bites on industrial batteries above 2 kWh (except those with only external storage), electric-vehicle batteries and SLI batteries. To prove a share, an operator has to trace the metal back through the cells, which is why recycled content and due diligence tend to travel together.

## The battery passport

From 18 February 2027, each LMT battery, each industrial battery above 2 kWh and each electric-vehicle battery that is placed on the market or put into service needs an electronic record, the [battery passport](/blog/battery-passport) (Article 77). It is the first digital product passport in the EU to become mandatory.

- The QR code leads to a unique identifier that the economic operator attributes. The identifier follows the ISO/IEC 15459 standards.
- Some data is public. Some is for notified bodies, market surveillance authorities and the Commission. Some is for people with a legitimate interest.
- The operator must keep the passport accurate, complete and up to date. It may authorise another operator in writing to act for it.
- The passport ceases to exist once the battery has been recycled.

One piece is late. Article 77(9) gave the Commission until 18 August 2026 to say who may read the restricted data. That deadline passed. The Commission opened a draft for feedback in October 2026. The passport date did not move. For the wider picture, see our update on [what changed and what is next](/blog/digital-product-passport-latest-updates).

## What could still change

Two files could change the rules. Neither is adopted.

**Proposal COM(2025) 981.** On 10 December 2025 the Commission proposed [targeted simplification](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52025PC0981) of the regulation. As we read it, it touches the producer definition for distance sales, the labelling of substances of concern, and LMT replaceability at module rather than cell level. It does not amend Article 77 or the QR code.

The Council agreed its [negotiating position on 24 June 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/24/simplification-council-agrees-negotiating-stance-to-simplify-and-streamline-environmental-rules/). It cuts redundant reporting, narrows the substance-of-concern idea, and adds an 18-month postponement of the removability and replaceability rules for certain product categories. As far as we can tell, Parliament has not finished its work. Do not plan around these changes until they appear in the Official Journal.

**The Omnibus IV file.** Procedure [2025/0130(COD)](https://bill.mino.law/bills/2025-0130-COD) is, on that tracker, in negotiation between Parliament and Council. It would exempt more battery operators from due diligence and move the reports from yearly to every three years. We could not verify the current state of those talks.

## A worked example: importing EV batteries

Say a company imports electric-vehicle batteries into the EU from a factory outside the bloc. It is the importer, so it is the economic operator. The duties are its own.

Because these are EV batteries, several duties apply at once:

- It must declare the carbon footprint of each battery model once the Commission's method applies.
- It must document recycled content, and meet the minimums for cobalt, lead, lithium and nickel from 2031.
- If its net turnover is above EUR 40 million, or it sits in a group above that line, it must run supply-chain due diligence from 18 August 2027.
- It must put a battery passport behind the QR code on each battery from 18 February 2027.

Miss any one of these and the battery is not ready for the EU market. The label dates depend on the implementing act, so confirm them before you print.

## The dates that matter

The regulation phases in over several years. This is the timeline to plan against:

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| 17 August 2023 | The regulation enters into force. |
| 18 February 2024 | Most rules start to apply. |
| 18 August 2025 | The separate collection symbol is required. |
| 18 August 2026 | Earliest date for the general labels, plus the deadline for the passport access-rights act. The deadline passed. |
| 18 February 2027 | The QR code, the battery passport and the removability rules apply. |
| 18 August 2027 | Supply-chain due diligence applies. |
| 18 August 2028 | Earliest date for recycled-content documentation and for the LMT carbon footprint. |
| 18 August 2031 | The recycled-content minimums apply. |

## FAQ

**What is the EU Batteries Regulation?**
It is Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, the EU law covering every battery placed on the EU market. It sets rules on carbon footprint, recycled content, supply-chain due diligence, labelling, collection and recycling, and the battery passport. It replaced the 2006 Batteries Directive, Directive 2006/66/EC.

Source: [EUR-Lex: EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng)

**When did the EU Batteries Regulation come into force?**
It entered into force on 17 August 2023 and has applied since 18 February 2024. Individual obligations phase in on later dates through to 2031.

Source: [EUR-Lex: EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Article 96](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng)

**When does battery due diligence apply?**
From 18 August 2027. Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 moved the date from 18 August 2025. Operators with a net turnover below EUR 40 million that are not part of a larger group are outside the chapter.

Source: [EUR-Lex: Regulation (EU) 2025/1561](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/1561/oj)

**What are the recycled-content requirements?**
Industrial batteries above 2 kWh, electric-vehicle batteries and SLI batteries must contain minimum recycled shares of key metals. From 18 August 2031 the minimums are 16% cobalt, 85% lead, 6% lithium and 6% nickel, rising further from 2036.

Source: [EUR-Lex: EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Article 8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng)

**Which batteries need a carbon footprint declaration?**
Electric-vehicle batteries, rechargeable industrial batteries above 2 kWh and LMT batteries. The start date is the later of a fixed date and 12 or 18 months after the Commission's act on the calculation method.

Source: [EUR-Lex: EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Article 7](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng)

**Who has to comply with the EU Batteries Regulation?**
The economic operators that make, import or distribute batteries on the EU market. The specific duties depend on the battery category (portable, LMT, industrial, electric-vehicle, or SLI).

Source: [EUR-Lex: EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng)

**Does the EU Batteries Regulation require a battery passport?**
Yes. LMT batteries, industrial batteries above 2 kWh and electric-vehicle batteries need a battery passport from 18 February 2027, accessed through a QR code on each battery.

Source: [EUR-Lex: EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Article 77](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/eng)

**Has the Batteries Regulation been amended?**
Once so far: Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 moved the due diligence date. A further proposal, COM(2025) 981 of 10 December 2025, is in the legislative process and is not adopted.

Source: [EUR-Lex: COM(2025) 981](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52025PC0981)
